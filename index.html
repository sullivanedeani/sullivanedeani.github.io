Sullivan K. Edeani

I am a PhD student in Political Science at the University of Kentucky, specializing in International Relations and Comparative Politics.

My research examines interstate conflict, maritime security, strategic vulnerability, and the domestic political consequences of violence. I am particularly interested in how the geographic context of conflict shapes state behavior, political institutions, and citizens’ relationships with government.

My ongoing projects investigate the relationship between farmer–herder violence and institutional trust in Nigeria, geographic domains of interstate conflict and state repression, and maritime chokepoints and interstate security.

View my academic CV for further information about my research and experience.

Research

Farmer–Herder Conflict and Institutional Legitimacy: Evidence from Nigeria
Working paper

Examines whether exposure to recurring local violence shapes trust in state institutions relative to traditional and religious authorities.

Where States Fight Matters: The Geography of Interstate Conflict and Domestic Repression
Working paper

Investigates whether land and maritime interstate conflicts are associated with different domestic repression responses.

Where Trade Travels Matters: Maritime Chokepoints and Interstate Conflict
Research in progress

Studies strategic vulnerability along maritime trade routes and its relationship to interstate conflict.
